# Generative world models as of September 7, 2026

Prepared for the development of Worldline. This review covers interactive video, persistent 3D generation, learned state dynamics, efficient training, and evaluation. It includes recent and less widely discussed research. It is a broad review of accessible primary sources, not a claim to have found every paper or private system.

## What the evidence supports

The field now has systems that generate convincing interactive video, systems that create persistent 3D scenes, and compact models that predict useful actions or physical state. These capabilities overlap, but none should be inferred from the others. A beautiful video can ignore a user's actions. A persistent mesh can have no learned dynamics. A useful robot planning model can produce no displayable images at all.

The strongest September announcements include **Runway GWM Worlds 2** and **World Labs Atlas**. The most relevant recent releases for reproducible engineering include **Matrix-Game 3.0**, **AlayaWorld v1.1**, **InSpatio-WorldFM**, **LingBot-World Infinity**, and **MMBench2**. Their release completeness and licensing differ substantially. This review found no evidence that a short original training run on a 24 GB laptop can achieve the visual range, action range, and long-horizon behavior of Genie 3.

The accompanying source catalogs provide dates, claims, limitations, release evidence and links for more than 40 model and research entries:

## What the newest research changes

**State and geometry are becoming explicit.** Marionette separates predicted state, geometric rendering and appearance generation. VGGT-World predicts geometry features, while AlayaWorld v1.1 renders a streaming point cache into future camera views. This makes camera control and scene persistence easier to inspect, but a renderer or a state repair rule must not be misrepresented as learned physics. Details and primary papers are in the [frontier catalog](source-catalog-frontier.md).

**Training must expose models to their own mistakes.** Self Forcing, Causal Forcing, Matrix's residual reinjection, Lyra's degraded-history training and several recent pipelines address the mismatch between clean training context and imperfect generated context. These approaches are established work. A new system needs an ablation showing a further benefit, with the same data and compute, before claiming a new advance.

**Action use needs direct tests.** CAER and CoCo explicitly investigate whether action-conditioned models actually change the relevant outcomes. MMBench2 studies action ignoring, visual hallucination and scene drift through task coverage and targeted data collection. Small one-step prediction error can coexist with failed long-term control. This is why Worldline's first evaluation separates generation, action-dependent dynamics and software persistence.

**New low-dimensional and object-centric models are worth following.** September's Modeling What Changes, physically grounded JEPA, and H3-World offer different routes to efficient control. Their reported scope ranges from small MuJoCo tasks to adapting a 33B video foundation. Low trainable parameter counts do not erase the memory and pretraining cost of the foundation. See the [architecture evidence](architecture-evidence.md) and [frontier catalog](source-catalog-frontier.md).

**Session persistence already has direct prior art.** PCS stores model/runtime/RNG state for replay and branching. WorldMem, DecMem and Lyra address visual memory through different representations. Exact restoration of saved data is useful, but it does not show that the model predicts a physically correct future. Neither persistence nor branching can honestly be claimed as a new scientific invention here.

## How to compare models without misleading numbers

[WorldMark v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21686v2), revised August 5, 2026, standardizes action mappings over 500 cases and ten models. It measures direction accuracy, motion purity, response latency and stability alongside world memory and visual quality. An important result is that high visual quality and accurate action following do not necessarily coincide. The current version covers more models and control diagnostics than the original April abstract.

[WBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25874) contains 289 cases and 1,058 interaction turns with quality, setting, interaction, consistency and physics dimensions. [Omni-WorldBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22212) focuses on interaction effects in space and time. WorldExam adds a broader reactivity perspective. These benchmarks should inform the eventual comparison, with exact model versions, controls and settings disclosed.

A defensible Genie comparison needs:

1. Matched tasks and equivalent action semantics, with first-person and third-person cases kept separate.
2. Held-out environments, prompts and trajectories, including action reversals, returns after occlusion, persistent edits and multiple interacting objects.
3. Actual action-to-observation latency, cold start, generation throughput, resolution, model steps, GPU count, memory and storage.
4. Blinded human visual preference tests with enough samples and confidence intervals, in addition to automated quality scores.
5. Multi-turn control and physics scores, failure cases and full unselected rollouts.
6. Explicitly separate rendered frames from newly inferred frames. Interpolation, meshes and shader animation must be identified.

No such head-to-head evaluation has been completed for Worldline. Its current evaluation only supports claims about the narrow local prototype.

## Open source, weights and training data

There are four distinct questions: whether code is available, whether weights are available, whether a working training recipe is available, and whether the required data can be obtained and used for the intended purpose. A public repository answers only the first question.

The catalogs flag specific issues: gated weights, absent raw video, withheld instruction data, missing training stages, noncommercial conditions, geographic exclusions and model-training restrictions. Released artifacts must be inspected individually. A paper's arXiv license does not license its model, and a permissive inference repository does not change an upstream checkpoint's terms.

For this first Worldline release, original code and original synthetic training data avoid inheriting external generative-model restrictions. This makes the training process reproducible at small scale. It also limits what the learned model can know: the synthetic terrain/ecology domain does not contain real-world appearance or general physical interactions.

## What Worldline is building, and what it is not yet

The first implementation trains a small conditional neural flow model to generate height and vegetation fields, and a separate neural model to predict water, vegetation and heat changes under user controls. A browser renders those fields as a navigable 3D landscape. Saved snapshots, local brush edits and branches are explicit software features.

This is an original structured generative prototype, with no inference API dependency. It is not an end-to-end video foundation model. Three terrain categories and a small keyword parser do not provide open-ended text understanding. The graphics contain programmed geometry, materials, water and foliage. The learned dynamics imitate an original synthetic teacher and do not establish real-world physical accuracy.

The immediate useful targets are exact persistence, spatially contained edits, and inspectable alternate futures from the same saved state. Those targets are measurable. Three revolutionary high-impact contributions, broad world generation and Genie 3 parity remain research goals. They require evidence beyond implementing familiar features.

## Resource decision

Local hardware inspected: Apple M4 Pro, 24 GB shared memory, 20 GPU cores, working PyTorch MPS. No external GPU account credentials were present in the task environment. No paid compute was provisioned.

The [Runpod pricing page](https://www.runpod.io/pricing), checked September 7, lists H100 PCIe at $2.89/hour and H100 SXM at $3.49/hour for the displayed dedicated configuration. As an illustrative arithmetic budget, eight SXM GPUs for 24 hours cost **$670.08 in GPU time alone**, before storage, tax and any other charges. This is not a prediction that one day or one such node can train a Genie-quality foundation. Availability and the final purchase screen require verification.

A sensible cloud experiment begins after local correctness and learning tests pass. Its purpose should be a specific held-out improvement or baseline reproduction. A full-scale training commitment needs a data plan, a parameter/sequence budget, a pilot throughput measurement, checkpoint/recovery rules and a spending cap. Increasing compute without those does not establish novelty or quality.

## Evidence limits and stopping decision

The search covered foundational 2022-2025 work and targeted 2026 releases through September 7, including relevant September 1-3 papers. Technical claims rely on primary papers, official project pages, repositories or model cards. Some sources are company claims or fresh preprints, and are labeled accordingly. We did not independently reproduce the external benchmark results, audit every cited repository or obtain private training details.

The research phase stopped broad discovery when it had enough current reference systems, directly relevant prior art, reproducibility evidence and evaluation methods to choose an honest implementation. A later scientific novelty claim would need a further targeted review and external experiments. This report does not claim exhaustive knowledge of unpublished or inaccessible research.
